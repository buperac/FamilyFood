## 1. Mild Butter Chicken Meatballs over Rice + Cucumber

**Why it's a candidate**: meatball texture is much easier for the 4yo than chunks of chicken. Mild butter chicken / tikka masala sauce has tomato + cream familiarity. Soft food = kid friendly.

**Sketch**: 1.5 lb ground chicken (or turkey) + breadcrumbs + egg + garlic + ginger + salt. Bake meatballs 18 min at 400°F. Simmer in mild jarred butter chicken or tikka masala sauce. Serve over basmati rice with sliced cucumber on the side (cool, crunchy palate cleanser).

**Calories**: ~620/adult
**Kid risk**: Low-Medium — cream sauce + meatball format does most of the work
**Cost**: ~$22-26 per meal
**Time**: 35 min

---

## 2. Breakfast-for-Dinner Egg Hash

**Why it's a candidate**: Highest kid-acceptance ceiling in the week. Cheap. Great after a meat-heavy week. Sometimes the 4yo eats more at "breakfast" than at any other meal.

**Sketch**: Diced potatoes pan-fried until crispy. Add diced onion, bell pepper, optional bacon or breakfast sausage. Crack 6-8 eggs over the top, lid on, cook until eggs are set. Top with shredded cheddar. Toast + cut fruit on the side.

**Calories**: ~550/adult
**Kid risk**: Very Low — eggs + potatoes + cheese is the kid trifecta
**Cost**: ~$12-16 per meal (cheapest of the week)
**Time**: 30 min

---

## 3. Perogy Skillet with Sour Cream and Green Peas

**Why it's a candidate**: Prairie-appropriate (you're in Alberta), very fast, soft pillowy texture that little kids love. Frozen perogies from Costco (cheddar-potato or potato-bacon) keep on hand.

**Sketch**: Boil 1 large bag (1 kg) of frozen perogies 4 min. Drain. Pan-fry in butter with sliced onion until golden, 6-8 min. Add a handful of frozen peas in the last 2 min. Top with sour cream + chopped chives. Side: simple green salad or coleslaw.

**Calories**: ~650/adult (denser carbs)
**Kid risk**: Very Low
**Cost**: ~$15-18 per meal
**Time**: 25 min

---

## 4. Red Lentil Sloppy Joes on Buns + Raw Veg Sticks

**Why it's a candidate**: Vegetarian night that doesn't taste vegetarian. Lentils disappear into tomato + Worcestershire base. Way cheaper than ground beef. Bun + ground-meat-style format = kid-recognized.

**Sketch**: Sauté onion + bell pepper + garlic. Add 1.5 cups red lentils + 4 cups veg broth + 1 can crushed tomatoes + 2 tbsp Worcestershire + 1 tbsp brown sugar + 1 tbsp Dijon + smoked paprika + salt. Simmer 20-25 min until lentils break down and it looks like sloppy joe filling. Serve on hamburger buns. Side: raw carrots, cucumber, celery sticks for crunch.

**Calories**: ~520/adult
**Kid risk**: Medium — depends on whether they accept the lentil texture; many kids do because it looks like meat sauce
**Cost**: ~$10-14 per meal (very cheap, mostly pantry)
**Time**: 35 min
